About the Content Ratings policy

Content ratings on Google Play are provided by the International Age Rating Coalition (IARC) and are designed to help developers communicate locally relevant content ratings to users. Regional IARC authorities maintain guidelines which are used to determine the maturity level of the content in an app. We don’t allow apps without a content rating on Google Play. Content ratings are used to inform consumers, especially parents, of potentially objectionable content that exists within an app.

You have two different paths for an appeal, depending on whether you disagree with the rating assigned by IARC or a policy decision made by Google.

To appeal the rating assigned by IARC:

You should follow this path if you believe the content rating assigned by IARC does not accurately reflect the answers you provided in the questionnaire (e.g., you believe your app should be rated E but received a T). IARC does not receive your app information until it is published. Once your app is live, you will get a "Live Rating Notice" email, and you can appeal your rating directly to IARC using the link provided in that email.

To appeal a policy violation decision made by Google:

You should follow this path if Google has flagged your app for having an inaccurate age rating and you believe this decision is incorrect (e.g., you answered the questionnaire honestly to receive an E rating, but Google’s review team believes your app is a T and has taken enforcement action).

If you agree with Google's feedback and have fixed the violation, you can directly submit an updated version of your app without an appeal. If you believe Google's decision is incorrect, you can appeal the violation decision. It may take up to 7 days, or longer in exceptional cases, to receive a response.
